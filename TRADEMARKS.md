## Using the marks

Write ™ after each mark until it registers with the UK Intellectual Property Office. Once a mark
registers, write ® after it instead. None of the four is registered yet, so all four carry ™ today.

A mark is always an adjective in front of a noun, never the noun itself: "the DIYA-GL engine", not
"a DIYA-GL"; "a DIY Accounting Submit filing", not "a DIY Accounting Submit". Keep the spelling
exact, and never pluralise a mark or make it possessive: "the DIYA-GL file format", not "DIYA-GL's
file format" or "two DIYA-GLs".

If you build software that reads or writes our files, name us the way this file does: keep the
mark's spelling, keep the noun after it, and say plainly that it is our product, not yours. "Reads
DIYA-GL files" or "exports to DIY Accounting Submit" works in a README, a settings screen, or an
error message. Do not shorten a mark, restyle it, or drop the noun it qualifies to make your own
branding read as part of ours.
