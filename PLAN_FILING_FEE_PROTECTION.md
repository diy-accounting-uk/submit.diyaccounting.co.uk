## Why

A filing fee is money Submit pays to Companies House from its own presenter credit account before the customer's payment can be clawed back. A confirmation statement costs the customer £61.35, of which £50 is Companies House's fee; a change of company name carries Companies House's standard or same-day fee. Today every Stripe dispute is accepted at once (`app/functions/billing/billingWebhookPost.js`, the `charge.dispute.created` case, `stripeClient.disputes.close`), the dispute flags only a subscription record, and any signed-in account with the right bundle can pay a fee the moment it signs up. A fraudster with stolen cards could file and leave Submit with the Companies House fees, the refunds and Stripe's dispute fees.

The fee-bearing activities today: the confirmation statement (`STRIPE_PRICE_ID_FILE_CONFIRMATION_STATEMENT`, `app/functions/companies-house/companiesHouseConfirmationStatementPost.js`, the 402 "pay before submitting" at line ~358) and the change of company name, standard and same day (`STRIPE_PRICE_ID_CHANGE_COMPANY_NAME_STANDARD` and `_SAME_DAY`, `app/services/companiesHouseFormFiling.js` `feeChoice` and `paidCharge`, line ~185). Fees are bought through `app/functions/billing/billingActivityCheckoutPost.js` and marked used on Companies House's acceptance (`app/services/companiesHouseSubmissionStatus.js`).

## The rules

The aim is to keep disputes from happening, more than to win them: card schemes and Stripe judge a seller by its dispute ratio, and at a few dozen payments a month two disputes can cross the monitoring threshold for the one Stripe account that also takes every subscription. Rules 3, 4, 5, 7 and 8 stop a doubtful account or card before it can pay a fee; rule 1 recovers money after a dispute has already counted.

1. **Contest filing-fee disputes; accept subscription disputes.** A dispute on a fee payment whose filing Companies House accepted is contested automatically with the evidence: the submission number and acceptance time, the receipt, the account's sign-in record (provider and time, no IP address stored beyond what the logs already hold), and the terms' fee and refund clauses. A fee payment whose filing was never accepted is refunded and the dispute accepted. A subscription dispute keeps today's auto-accept.
2. **Any dispute or early fraud warning holds the account.** On `charge.dispute.created` and `radar.early_fraud_warning.created` for any of the account's payments, the account is held: no fee checkout and no fee-bearing filing until every dispute on it has closed. A closed dispute that the customer won (Submit lost) keeps the hold until the operator lifts it.
3. **Paid subscribers only.** A fee checkout and a fee-bearing filing need a live paid Stripe subscription (Resident Ltd, Resident Pro or Resident Tax Agent, monthly or yearly). A bundle from a pass (the comp, guest and invited bundles) does not qualify. Accounts on the operator list (`app/lib` operator emails) are exempt from this rule only.
4. **Seasoning and the cooling-off.** A subscriber is *unseasoned* until their subscription is 90 days old or has renewed once. An unseasoned subscriber is not offered a fee filing until 72 hours after their subscription's first successful payment: the page says from when they can file, and no fee is taken before then. Once a fee is paid, the filing goes to Companies House at once; Submit never takes a fee for a filing it then holds (operator, 2026-11-26: "we can't process the filing we shouldn't offer it and hold"). Seasoned subscribers file at once. The hours and days are settings per environment.
5. **Caps.** Per subscriber: £150 of fee payments in any rolling 30 days. Per environment: £150 of fee payments by *unseasoned* subscribers together in any rolling 30 days, so a fraudster, or a busy week of new customers, cannot close fee filing for established customers. Both are settings per environment and can differ later; both count the customer's payment (£61.35 for a confirmation statement), not only Companies House's share. A refused payment says which cap and when it lifts.
6. **Authenticate every fee payment.** The fee checkout asks Stripe for card authentication on every payment (`payment_method_options.card.request_three_d_secure: "any"`), so an authenticated fraudulent payment's chargeback is normally the card issuer's loss.
7. **The same card, a clean card.** While a subscriber is unseasoned, the fee must be paid with the card that pays the subscription (Stripe's card `fingerprint` on both payment methods), and a card whose fingerprint has paid for another Submit account is refused.
8. **Stripe's risk score.** A fee charge whose `outcome.risk_level` is `elevated` or `highest` is refunded at once (the fee is unspent, so nothing is lost but Stripe's processing fee) and the account held.
9. **Visible to the operator.** The operator dashboard shows the fee payments in the window against both caps, the subscribers nearest their cap, the held accounts and the open disputes and warnings. The operator gets an email (the same mechanism as the subscriber copy, `OPERATOR_COPY_KINDS` in `app/functions/email/customerEmailSend.js`) for each fee payment, each cap reached (and at 80% of the environment cap), each dispute and each early fraud warning.

### FFP1. Disputes and early fraud warnings

In `billingWebhookPost.js`: for `charge.dispute.created`, find the payment's kind from the checkout session or payment intent metadata (the activity checkout records the activity id and subject key; read `billingActivityCheckoutPost.js` for the field names). A fee payment whose charge is marked used (`markChargeUsed`) is contested: build the evidence from the Companies House receipt (submission number, acceptance time, company number) and the account record, and submit it with `stripe.disputes.update(id, { evidence, submit: true })` (a new `app/services/disputeEvidence.js`, unit-tested with a recorded dispute object). A fee payment that is not used is refunded through the existing refund path and the dispute accepted. A subscription payment keeps the auto-accept. For every dispute and for `radar.early_fraud_warning.created` (add it to the webhook's events in `infra/stripe/stripe-sync.js` or wherever the endpoint's enabled events are set), write a hold on the account (an attribute on the account's record, keyed by the hashed sub, queried by key, never scanned) with the dispute or warning id; `charge.dispute.closed` with status `won` (Submit won) clears that id's hold; `lost` keeps it for the operator. Publish activity events `fee-dispute-contested`, `account-held`, `account-hold-cleared`. Before choosing to contest, read Stripe's current UK terms for the dispute fee (about £20) and whether a won dispute returns it or contesting adds a fee, and record them in this plan. An authenticated fee payment's fraud claim is normally the issuer's; the disputes Submit sees on those are "not received" or "not as described", which the acceptance evidence answers. Visa's Compelling Evidence 3.0 needs two undisputed payments from the same customer 120 to 365 days old, which a new subscriber cannot have. An early fraud warning holds the account and emails the operator; it does not refund (operator, 2026-11-26). Gates: `npm test`, the webhook unit tests extended for each branch.

### FFP2. The fee gate

One module (`app/services/filingFeeGate.js`, new) answers "may this account pay this fee now?" with a reason and a time it lifts: the hold (FFP1), a live paid Stripe subscription (rule 3, with the operator exemption), the cooling-off from the subscription's first successful payment (rule 4), and the two caps over the rolling 30 days (rule 5). Both `billingActivityCheckoutPost.js` (before a fee checkout) and the two filing paths (`companiesHouseConfirmationStatementPost.js`, `companiesHouseFormFiling.js`) call it, so a fee bought before a hold or a cap still cannot file while the account is held. The caps need the fee payments by account and by environment over 30 days without a Scan: record each paid fee in a small ledger table with the account as key and a date sort key, plus one item per environment and day holding that day's total (atomic `ADD`), so the environment total is 30 key reads; the table goes in `DataStack.java` with its names in `DataNames.java` and the counts in `infra/test/java` named "from -> to". Settings per environment through the stack's Lambda environment: `FILING_FEE_COOLING_OFF_HOURS` (72), `FILING_FEE_CAP_PENCE_PER_SUBSCRIBER` (15000), `FILING_FEE_CAP_PENCE_PER_ENVIRONMENT` (15000), `FILING_FEE_CAP_WINDOW_DAYS` (30). The fee checkout session sets `payment_method_options.card.request_three_d_secure: "any"`. Rules 4, 7 and 8: the gate reads the subscriber's seasoning from the subscription record and refuses a fee checkout before the cooling-off ends; the card check compares the fee checkout's payment method `card.fingerprint` with the subscription's default payment method and with a fingerprint index (key: fingerprint, value: hashed sub; Query by key); the risk check reads `charge.outcome.risk_level` in the webhook's `checkout.session.completed` for a fee and refunds through the existing refund path. The refusal is a 403 JSON with `code` (`fee-account-held`, `fee-needs-paid-subscription`, `fee-cooling-off`, `fee-subscriber-cap`, `fee-environment-cap`) and `until` where it applies. The simulator and the mock billing (`app/functions/non-lambda-mocks/mockBilling.js`) honour the gate. Gates: `npm test`, `./mvnw clean verify`, `npm run test:fileConfirmationStatementBehaviour-simulator` with a step for each refusal.

### FFP4. The pages, terms, FAQ and Welsh

The confirmation statement and change of company name pages show each refusal in plain words before the person pays: "Filing fees are for paid subscribers", "Your first filing fee can be paid from <date and time>", "You have paid £X in filing fees in the last 30 days, the limit is £150; you can pay again from <date>", "Filing fees are paused across Submit until <date>", "Your account is on hold while a payment dispute is open". The terms' section 10 (fees and refunds) states the rules: paid subscribers only, the cooling-off, the caps, card authentication, that a disputed fee payment for an accepted filing is contested with the filing's evidence, and that an account with an open dispute cannot pay fees. The FAQ gains one answer. `privacy.html` says what Submit sends Stripe as dispute evidence (the filing's submission number, acceptance time and receipt, the account's sign-in provider and time) and the card fingerprint check. The page shows, before any payment, from when an unseasoned subscriber can file. Welsh for every new string. Gates: the pages' browser tests, `npm test`, `npm run build:welsh`, the Welsh proof in bramble's look wave.
